● All ten, answered from the ledgers (numbers verified against
  distortion_remodel_request.md, pipeline-version-state, real_row_radii_v54.txt,
  and the d0/ms ledgers):

  1. "Tier 2" is our ledger shorthand, not standard HEP jargon — my coinage
  during the d0 diagnostic, later adopted by the pipeline's ledger; I should
  have flagged it. Tier 1 = the d0 structures the field model class reproduces
  and that were sized to measurement (the ~3 cm d0 RMS, its cosine
  isotropy/null, the membrane jump, stiff circle RMS). Tier 2 = the one residual
  that class provably cannot produce: the d0-median vs drift-time profile
  (+1.4/+1.8 cm shifts in specific tbin bins, from the d0 diagnostic). The
  pipeline's probe ladder showed that any displacement field constant along
  drift moves d0 without tbin structure — reproducing that profile needs a
  drift-coupled mechanism (space charge evolving along drift, E×B, z-dependent
  distortion). Hence "tier-2, open."

  2. The map question is closed by measurement; the noise-fitting worry is 
  legitimate but guarded against. Row-lock (every layer's clusters at exactly
  one radius — real_row_radii_v54.txt) proves no distortion correction was
  applied to the real clusters. The measured per-layer radius offsets are
  mm-scale (+1.2 mm at layer 7, shrinking outward) versus the CDB static map's
  predicted +1.0–1.6 cm bulk — ten times smaller and differently shaped — and
  the real d0-cosine is null, excluding bulk translation. So the map is neither
  applied nor a usable mechanism model, which is why v5.4 was sized to run
  79507's own meters. On noise-fitting: the model has ~4 amplitudes against 6+
  independent summary observables (stiff RMS ratio, d0 RMS and median, cosine
  null, CM jump 0.480±0.13, side-crosser fraction, split-rate), all matched
  within measured errors, none fit per-event; and the independent half-arc
  curvature meter shows sim 0.276 vs real 0.402 — the model undershoots real
  structure, the opposite signature of overfitting. The residual concern is
  provenance, not noise: v5.4c models this file as produced (uncorrected). If
  the collaboration ever reprocesses run 79507 with corrections on, the overlay
  must be re-sized (same meters, cheap). Whether "uncorrected" is intended for
  this run era is a genuine team question — it sits next to the measured-σ_z
  question already on the pipeline ledger.

  3. Same underlying fact as your earlier question, but it's the exclusion 
  argument, not the root cause. Your earlier point was bookkeeping: truth stores
  one scalar pT while the trajectory is many positions. This statement is about
  model expressiveness: a positions-only field displaces clusters by location,
  blind to which track they belong to, so it adds essentially the same
  quadrature smear to soft and stiff tracks and cannot manufacture a
  momentum-dependent pattern. Since the post-v5.4c ratio is momentum-structured
  (0.82 soft, 0.99 stiff), the residual cannot be the field — it must live in
  cluster response, which is momentum-coupled through crossing angle and drift.
  The root cause of med05 itself is the sim's soft-track cluster width (question
  6).

  4. "Stiff" is conventional HEP jargon — a high-momentum, small-curvature track
  ("hard to bend"; opposite "soft"). In our ledgers it means the pT 1.5–2.5 GeV
  window. Stiff tracks are the distortion probes because at 2 GeV, R = 476 cm
  and the sagitta over the pad-row chord is only ~5 mm, so mm-to-cm distortion
  competes directly with the signal, while MS and intrinsic curvature both
  shrink as 1/p.

  5. The arithmetic was right both times; the v5.3-era interpretation was wrong 
  — and your quadrature formula is exactly the corrected accounting, confirmed 
  numerically by v5.4c. The old 0.94 was real/sim = 692/733, a correct ratio of
  what sim measured then — but sim was incomplete: missing the ~426 µm
  distortion smear (too narrow) while carrying a too-wide base response (too
  wide), two offsetting errors netting to false agreement. Your formula: √(733²
  + 426²) = 848 µm predicted, vs 842 measured on v5.4c (the small difference is
  the R-window reshuffle, noted in the pipeline ledger) → 692/848 = 0.82,
  today's ratio. So the method wasn't wrong from the start — the mistake was
  reading 0.94 as "response matches" without asking whether both sides contained
  the same physics. The tell was the stiff window, where the cancellation
  failed loudly (1.31): that number is what launched the whole distortion
  campaign.

  6. Not solved by v5.4c — deliberately. v5.4c touches only positions (that
  constraint is what kept labels bit-exact); it added the missing distortion
  term and thereby exposed the base mismatch (0.94 → 0.82). Solving it is
  digitization/clustering-response work on the pipeline side. Our numbers
  localize it usefully: the stiff-window base widths already agree (sim 476 µm
  measured directly at v5.3; real inferred √(626² − ~420²) ≈ 460–480), while the
  soft-window bases disagree by 30% (sim 733–735 vs real ~564). An excess that
  appears only for soft tracks points at the
  crossing-angle/track-inclination-dependent width term (soft tracks cross pads
  at larger angles), not the angle-independent floor (diffusion/pad-response
  width, which stiff tracks sample and which matches). Caveat: the inferred
  bases inherit the ~415–426 µm smear estimate's uncertainty; the mechanism hunt
  belongs to the pipeline session.

  7. No — not closed. The latest pipeline record (v5.4c, 2026-08-06) seals the
  field axis and explicitly carries "open items unchanged: med05, tbin-median
  profile." The "re-solve the field amplitude" line is the recorded plan-shape
  for when a response fix lands — narrowing the base changes total stiff RMS, so
  SMOD must be re-sized against the stiff meter; the two knobs are
  quadrature-coupled. No response work has landed. It's gated on your
  relay/direction.

  8. Side-sharing is the opposite of overfitting — it removed free parameters.
  v5.4b let each side's harmonics float independently, and the emergent membrane
  jump came out 2.5× the real one (that was one of my two surfacings). v5.4c
  constrains the harmonics to one shared set — continuous across the membrane,
  the physical expectation since the membrane is the only genuine discontinuity
  surface — and then adds the discontinuity explicitly as a single per-side term
  sized to the direct measurement (real med|J| 0.480±0.13 → sim 0.536,
  in-band). Fewer parameters, each anchored to a measurement, and the curvature
  meter still conservative: an under-fit, physically-shaped model.

  9. Yes — both remain pipeline-side beyond v5.4c, and this campaign added a 
  third. (a) med05/response (needs a digitization-level change, question 6); (b)
  the tier-2 d0-vs-drift-time profile (needs a drift-coupled mechanism class);
  (c) new from the symmetric scan: rate 0.48 — sim frames carry 2.1× the
  track-class circles at 0.88× the cluster occupancy, an
  organization/content-axis observation. v5.4c closed exactly one axis:
  cluster-position realism. All three are written up in ms_conclusion_v54c.md;
  relaying them is your call.

  10. No — and for the one use-case that would care, a rerun of GEANT still 
  isn't the right tool. The overlay is post-hoc by design (the pipeline's
  recorded decision: position-level work uses a cluster-position warp, not
  re-simulation), which is what bought bit-exact labels; truth hits should be
  field-free, since truth is the actual trajectory and the field models
  reconstruction-side displacement. The only consumer wanting field-realistic
  sim pixels is future pixel-level ML training — and there a coordinate warp is
  impossible in principle: pixels live on the pad/tbin grid, and real
  distortions act on the drifting charge before pad quantization, shifting
  charge-sharing between neighboring pads. The faithful version injects the
  displacement inside the digitization stage and re-digitizes (GEANT libraries
  unchanged — the cheap half of a rerun; labels then need re-verification).
  That's a decision to make only if/when pixel-level sim training starts and
  sub-pad realism matters (scale: 426 µm scatter ≈ half a pad; the 4.8 mm
  membrane jump ≈ 1–2 pads). For the current cluster-level plan — and for this
  campaign, which deliberately anchors sim pre-clustering on truth hits and
  bridges through the cluster chain — v5.4c is complete.

● Here's the layman version.

  The sim is an assembly line. Stage 1: Geant4 flies particles through the
  detector geometry (pure physics, "truth"). Stage 2: many collisions get
  stacked into a DAQ-style time frame, like the real detector sees. Stage 3 —
  digitization, the "digi": the detector response is simulated — each particle's
  charge drifts through the gas, spreads out, and lands on the pad plane,
  producing pixels: "pad number 517, time bin 213, charge 42." Each pixel gets
  stamped with which particle made it (the per-pixel truth ID). Stage 4:
  clustering glues touching pixels into clusters and computes each cluster's
  position. The cluster file is what the tracker and the ML consume.

  What v5.3 was: the era when the whole line finally got tuned end-to-end —
  right collision species, right amount of charge, right electronics behavior —
  and then frozen ("sealed") with checksums. v5.3 produced every stage's file:
  the pixel file (digi) and the cluster file.

  What v5.4 fixed: my circle-fit tests showed real tracks are slightly warped —
  they fit circles worse than sim at high momentum, they point 2.5 cm away from
  the beamline, there's a small jump at the TPC's central membrane. The real
  detector has distortions and misalignments; the sealed sim was too perfect.
  The fix, v5.4a/b/c (three iterations of the warp's shape), was applied in the
  cheapest, safest possible place: the very last stage. After clusters are
  built, each cluster's position gets nudged by the measured warp. Nothing else
  is touched. That was deliberate: it guaranteed every pixel, every truth label,
  every cluster's pixel membership stayed byte-identical to sealed v5.3 — so
  all the v5.3-era tuning and label quality carried over with zero risk, and I
  could verify it ("labels bit-exact") in a day.

  Why v5.4 has no pixel file of its own: look at where the nudge sits on the
  line — downstream of the pixels. Pixels → clustering → [v5.4 nudge] →
  clusters. The pixels of "v5.4c" are therefore literally the v5.3 pixel file; a
  "v54c digi" would be the identical bytes under a new name. It's like editing
  a finished photo in Photoshop: the edited print is new, but the negatives are
  the same negatives — there is no "edited negative."

  Why you can't just nudge the pixels the same way: a pixel is not a dot you can
  move — it's a square on graph paper. "Pad 517, time bin 213" has no position
  half a pad to the left; the grid is fixed hardware. In the real detector, the
  distortion happens to the drifting electron cloud while it's still in the gas,
  before it lands — so what actually changes is which pads receive how much 
  charge (the sharing between neighboring pads shifts a little, and that's what
  moves the cluster's computed position). To imitate that honestly you have to
  displace the charge before it's binned onto the pads — which means going
  inside stage 3 and re-running digitization. That's exactly the request you're
  relaying: same measured warp, injected one stage earlier, cluster-stage nudge
  switched off so the warp isn't applied twice.

  The price, and why it's a real decision rather than an afternoon job: once
  charge moves before binning, some of it crosses pad borders — pixels regroup
  slightly, so the labels will no longer be byte-identical, and the
  cluster-level checks all have to be re-verified. v5.4's overlay traded a
  little physical purity for perfect safety; the new production trades that
  safety back for pixel-level realism, so it comes with a verification campaign
  attached.

❯ that's enough. Then you're moving this correction from downstream to upper    
  stream - why not directly move it to .gdml, say decode the ideal .gdml, add   
  the "field" in it then recode it back for Geant4 - once and for all?          

● Because in a TPC, the geometry you'd be warping doesn't actually participate
  in the measurement — and baking the correction into the baseline would destroy
  the very reference that makes it measurable. Four reasons, in increasing
  order of importance:

  1. There's no object in the GDML to warp. The field is a per-(layer, sector,
  side) displacement of readout coordinates. But pads and rows don't exist in
  the GDML as geometry — the sensitive volume is one featureless gas cylinder,
  and the pad segmentation lives entirely in the readout/digitization software.
  You could move internal boundaries of uniform gas all day and Geant4 would
  deposit exactly the same charge in exactly the same places, because deposits
  depend on the gas, not on imaginary lines inside it. "Where the pads are"
  first becomes a meaningful concept at digitization — which is precisely why
  that's the stage that must carry the warp.

  2. Warping the GDML moves the wrong object: the world instead of the camera.
  Real particles fly clean circles through the real TPC — we proved the
  trajectory is a circle to 20 µm, and that's true in reality too, misalignment
  or not. Neither candidate mechanism touches the particle: mechanical
  misalignment means the pads report shifted coordinates; drift distortion means
  the signal electrons get deflected on their way to the pads. Both live
  between trajectory and pixels — in the camera, not the streets. Editing the
  GDML would warp the streets: truth trajectories would come from a distorted
  world, so truth would stop being truth. Every truth-anchored result — the
  circle test, the MS measurement, the ML labels' meaning of "where the particle
  really was" — would be silently corrupted. The current factorization (truth =
  ideal physics, response = everything the detector does to it) is exactly what
  a distortion-aware analysis or model needs preserved.

  3. The practical cost explodes. The GDML is the input to the most expensive
  sealed stage: the G4 collision libraries and every content/shape tune from
  v3.6 through v5.3 were validated on it. Change the geometry and all of that
  reopens. The digitization injection was designed to reuse the G4 libraries
  unchanged — that's the cheap half of the rerun, and it's only possible because
  the geometry stays put.

  4. "Once and for all" is exactly backwards — the field is a perishable, 
  run-specific number. Part of it is plausibly space charge, which varies with
  beam conditions; all of it is provisional on run 79507's uncorrected
  production state (if the collaboration reprocesses with corrections on, the
  field must be re-sized against the new files). Such a quantity belongs in a
  versioned, swappable, clearly-labeled stage — not fossilized into the one
  component that's supposed to be the stable, collaboration-shared ideal
  reference. And note the self-defeating loop: the field is defined as "measured
  real minus ideal." Bake it into the ideal, and the next measurement has no
  anchor — you'd be measuring residuals against your own previous correction,
  forever.

  So digitization isn't the compromise location — it's the physically correct
  one: the single stage that sits downstream of the particle physics (protecting
  truth), upstream of pad binning (letting the warp express itself as
  charge-sharing, like reality), covers both candidate mechanisms without
  choosing between them, and keeps the correction versioned beside a pristine
  reference. The GDML's job is to stay ideal so that "the discrepancy" remains a
  well-defined, re-measurable thing.
